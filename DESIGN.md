# Slate design rules

Rules learned from corrections on this project. Read this before any UI work. Each rule covers a kind of component, uses the project's own tokens, and carries the reason and the words that prompted it.

## Motion

- **A selector that flows is one thick body in the theme colour.** When an indicator moves like liquid (the dock selector in `components/dock-liquid.tsx`), draw it as a single continuous shape filled with `--primary`, with its label in `--primary-foreground`. No separate beads strung behind it, no middle thinner than its ends, no metal or other material outside the theme, and edges that stay smooth when captured at 3×.
  Why: the first version, a chrome bead trailing a string of smaller ones, "look[ed] extremely chunky".
  The parts it is built from must never be told apart, at any frame of a move. If it is assembled from pieces (the web's two ends and the span between them), every piece shares one height at every instant, so the outline is always a single capsule; it slims as a whole when stretched (at most about 16%) and never narrows in the middle. Where the platform can draw one shape (Compose, SwiftUI), draw one capsule from the trailing edge to the leading edge instead of pieces. Check by pausing a long move midway (first option to last) and looking at the outline.
  Why: with a thinner middle, "sometimes I can see the rectangle just for a bit".

- **Every control that moves a marker between options uses the liquid selector.** Segmented filters, view switches, the dock and any new tab-like control render `LiquidSelector` (`components/liquid-selector.tsx`; `SegmentedFilter` and `ViewSwitcher` already wrap it) instead of a sliding pill, a `layoutId` highlight or a colour swap. It can be dragged as well as tapped, and the chosen label's ink colour is drawn by the selector, not by the button. Use the `LIQUID_CONTROL` springs for small controls and `LIQUID_DOCK` only for the dock.
  Why: not given yet.
  <!-- 2026-11-14: "Can we add the gooey motion and behavior to everywhere on Slate?" -->

- **A choice among a few ordered settings is a slider you can throw.** When the options lie on a scale (Light, System, Dark), render them as stops on one track: icon-only stops, the chosen one's name shown only inside the thumb, a grab cursor, and the thumb follows a drag and is thrown to the nearest stop on release, landing with a visible overshoot (`LIQUID_SLIDER` springs on `LiquidSelector`). `ThemeSlider` in `components/appearance-controls.tsx` is the model. Plain tab-like choices (filters, views) stay segmented controls.
  Why: it should feel "more like smoother or polished", and a row of labelled segments did not say it could be slid.
  <!-- 2026-11-18: "Wondering if we could make it like a slider which goes through different stuff, like a horizontal slider, and add some like release nice motion, so it feels like a more like smoother or polished experience." (on the theme switcher's Light / System / Dark segmented control) -->

- **A colour choice is a colour you slide through, never a row of swatch circles.** Accent (and any future colour setting) is one track painted with the options blended in OKLab, in hue order, with a faint mark at each stop and a white-ringed thumb filled with the colour under it. While the thumb moves, the whole app takes that colour live (`--primary` and its partners set inline on `<html>`), so the choice is felt before it is made; on release the thumb is thrown to the nearest stop, lifts and settles with a soft bounce, and the stylesheet's class takes over with no visible step. `AccentSlider` in `components/accent-slider.tsx` is the model; its colours come from `ACCENT_PRIMARY` in `lib/accent-theme.ts`, which must stay in step with `html.accent-*` in `app/globals.css`. Blend in OKLab, never by hue: hue blending sweeps the wheel (amber to mint through neon lime) and reads as a generic hue picker.
  Why: a row of colour dots is "seen ... in a lot of AI slop generated designs"; the slider should have "buttery motion to invoke emotion".
  <!-- 2026-11-18: "I mean like the theme color slider thing with buttery motion to invoke emotion. That would have been nice, right? And that those circles, I have seen it, and a lot of AI slop generated designs." (on six 32px accent circles under the theme slider) -->

- **A change of the whole screen waits for the control, then fades evenly.** Switching theme (or anything that recolours the full page) is one view-transition crossfade of about 320 ms on an even curve (`cubic-bezier(0.45, 0, 0.25, 1)`), the new page fading in over an opaque still of the old. It starts only once the control's thumb has landed (about 300 ms), because the fade freezes a picture of the page and anything still moving would show twice. A choice that looks the same (Dark to System on a dark phone) gets no fade. Elements that animate on their own during it (the open theme button) get a `view-transition-name` and stay live above the fade. Scope the CSS with a class on `<html>`, never a view-transition type, which older Safari lacks. `useThemeSwitch` in `components/appearance-controls.tsx` is the model.
  Why: on an iPhone the switch looked "really glitchy"; it should be "smooth as fuck".
  <!-- 2026-11-18: "The switch between system dark and light is looking really glitchy when I checked on my iPhone, so make it smooth as fuck." (the 200 ms fade started while the thumb was mid-throw, so a frozen thumb and button icon crossfaded under live ones, and Dark to System on a dark phone still ran a fade) -->

- **Nothing arrives in a single frame.** Three parts, all already wired app-wide:
  - Images (posters, backdrops, covers, avatars) fade in once loaded and decoded. `ImageArrival` (`components/image-arrival.tsx`, mounted in the app and public-profile layouts) does this for every `<img>`; never add a per-component image pop or a second fade on top (opt out with `data-image-arrival="off"` only when an image runs its own crossfade).
  - A screen or a whole result set that replaces another fades up over about 220 ms starting from 45% opacity (`.app-tab-arrive`), never from 0: a new screen's first frame is its slowest, and starting from nothing shows an empty page for that long.
  - A control answers the tap before the work it triggers: the selector moves at once and the heavy re-render follows (`SegmentedFilter` hands the change to its owner one frame later).
  Why: "loading of all screens could be better. They just flash right now."

## Interaction

- **An icon button for a setting shows the setting chosen, not its effect.** Every choice must visibly change the closed button: the theme button shows a monitor for System, never the sun or moon System resolves to. The new icon turns into place (WAAPI, about 320 ms, `--ease-ui-out`).
  Why: picking System left the icon as it was, which read as broken.
  <!-- 2026-11-18: "when I'm setting the theme to system, it's the icon is not updating in the collapse state or like the button thing" -->

- **A card's actions sit in one row, and it is dismissed from its top-right corner.** On a phone, two or three actions share one row in equal widths (`grid grid-cols-2`), and a single action takes the whole row; from `sm` up they sit at their own width beside the text. Buttons are 44px tall on a phone (`h-11`), 40px from `sm` up (`h-10`). A card that can be dismissed has its close button (a 36px X) pinned to the top-right corner with the text padded clear of it; a card without an X gets a "Not now" text button in the action row instead. Never let buttons wrap onto a second line, centre them in a left-aligned card, or leave a close button floating beside them. `NudgeCard` and `ActionRow` in `components/library-nudges.tsx` follow this.
  Why: "Just basic UX stuff." Wrapped, centred buttons and a stray X made a card read as unfinished.
  The same holds for a page header's action on a phone (`components/public-list-view.tsx`): a single action spans the full width (`w-full sm:w-auto`) and nothing sits beside it, so a count such as "7 titles" shows only from `sm` up.
